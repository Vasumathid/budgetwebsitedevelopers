Website Design Cost in India (2026): What Restaurants, Shops & Startups Actually Pay

Updated August 2026 · 6 min read · By Vasumathi Dulasidaran

If you've asked three different developers "how much for a website?" and gotten three wildly different numbers, you're not alone. Website pricing in India ranges from ₹5,000 template jobs to ₹5,00,000+ enterprise builds — and almost nobody explains why. Here's a straight breakdown of what things actually cost and what drives the price.

Typical price ranges by business type

Business typeTypical rangeWhat's usually included
Small shop / cafe / freelancer₹15,000 – ₹25,0004-6 pages, mobile-friendly, contact form
Restaurant with menu/ordering₹20,000 – ₹35,000Menu display, WhatsApp/order links, gallery
Startup / professional₹25,000 – ₹50,000Portfolio, booking forms, blog setup
School / clinic / institution₹30,000 – ₹80,000Multi-department pages, staff directories, forms

Ranges reflect typical small-business projects in India as of 2026 and vary by developer, region and scope.

What actually drives the price up or down

1. Number of custom pages, not templates

A 5-page site built on a proper custom layout costs more than a 20-page site built from a drag-and-drop template — because you're paying for design work, not page count. If a quote is priced purely per page, ask what's actually being customized.

2. Functionality, not decoration

Booking forms, payment integration, multi-language support, and admin panels where you can edit content yourself all add real development time. A static "brochure" site with no moving parts is the cheapest tier for a reason.

3. Whether SEO setup is included

A site with proper meta tags, a sitemap, and mobile-first structure isn't just prettier — it's the difference between showing up on Google and being invisible. Ask explicitly whether this is included or a separate line item.

4. Ongoing hosting and domain costs

The build price is usually one-time. Domain registration (roughly ₹800–₹1,500/year) and hosting (₹0–₹5,000/year depending on traffic) are typically separate, recurring costs — make sure your quote is clear about which is which.

Red flag to watch for: a quote with no explanation of what's included, or one that seems too low to cover even basic mobile-responsive design and SEO setup. A site that isn't mobile-friendly can actively hurt you — most Indian traffic in 2026 is mobile-first, and Google ranks mobile-friendliness directly.

Is a cheap website actually worth it?

For most small businesses just starting out, yes — a well-built basic site is worth the investment. It's the difference between a customer finding you on Google versus only Instagram, and it signals legitimacy the way a shopfront does. It stops being worth it only when "cheap" means no mobile responsiveness, no SEO basics, or a template so generic it looks identical to a hundred other local businesses.
