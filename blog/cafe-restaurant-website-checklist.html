7 Things Every Restaurant & Cafe Website Needs Before You Open

Updated August 2026 · 5 min read · By Vasumathi Dulasidaran

Most restaurant websites fail at one job: getting a hungry, hurried person to decide "yes, here" in under 10 seconds. Here's exactly what needs to be on the site — and what's just decoration.

1 A menu that's actually readable, not a PDF

PDF menus are the single most common mistake — they're slow to load, impossible to read on a phone without endless pinch-zooming, and invisible to Google's search results. A menu built as real page content, with prices and categories, loads instantly and can even show up directly in Google search results.

2 Location and hours above the fold

Your address, opening hours, and a Google Maps embed should be visible without scrolling. Someone deciding where to eat right now doesn't want to hunt for this — if it's buried, they'll pick the competitor whose site shows it immediately.

3 A one-tap way to call or order

A phone number that's a real clickable link (`tel:`), and a WhatsApp or ordering link that opens directly — not a "contact us" form for something time-sensitive like a food order. Every extra step between "hungry" and "ordered" loses customers.

4 Real photos, not stock images

Actual photos of your food and your space consistently outperform generic stock photography for restaurants — people are deciding based on what the place and food actually look like, and stock photos are easy to spot and mildly distrust-inducing.

5 Mobile-first design, not "mobile-friendly"

There's a real difference. "Mobile-friendly" often means a desktop site that technically shrinks to fit a phone, with tiny tap targets and slow load times. "Mobile-first" means it was designed for a phone screen first — which is where the overwhelming majority of restaurant searches happen.

6 Google Business Profile connected, not just a website

A website alone isn't enough for local discovery — pairing it with a claimed, filled-out Google Business Profile (photos, hours, reviews) is what actually gets a restaurant to show up in Google Maps and the local "near me" search results.

7 Reviews or testimonials visible on the site itself

Don't make people leave your site to check reviews elsewhere — pulling a few genuine testimonials directly onto the homepage keeps a hesitant visitor on the page and moving toward a decision, instead of getting distracted checking a review site.

What you can skip, at least at first: online reservation systems, loyalty program integrations, and multi-language switchers are nice-to-haves — they add real cost and aren't what makes or breaks a first-time visitor's decision. Get the 7 above right first.
